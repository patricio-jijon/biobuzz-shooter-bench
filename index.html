          The two amber inputs are the ones you keep changing. Wheel radius multiplies directly
          into the exit speed — double the diameter at the same rpm and the ball leaves
          twice as fast. That is the whole reason a part swap changes where the ball lands.
        

      


      

        
The three rules
          what actually sets where the ball lands

        

          

            

              1 · A bigger wheel throws further

            

              The rim moves at ω × r. At a fixed rpm, doubling the diameter doubles
              the rim speed, so it doubles the ball speed. Range grows faster still, because a
              projectile’s reach goes roughly with the square of its speed.

            

          

          

            

              2 · More motors do not throw further

            

              Top speed is set by the motor’s free speed and the gear ratio, and every
              5203 shares the same free speed at 1:1. A second motor buys torque, not rpm:
              it reaches speed sooner and sags less per ball. Gear up to throw further; add
              motors to throw more often.

            

          

          

            

              3 · More wheels steady the shot

            

              Each ball steals energy from the flywheel. Spread across more flywheels (or a steel
              booster) the same ball costs a smaller share of the stored energy, so the rpm dips
              less and the next shot repeats. The cost is a longer spin-up.

One number here is an estimate, not a manual value. The height of the
          upward-facing CELL opening appears only in Figures 9-10/9-11, which are images. The slider is
          the height of the raised CELL’s mouth — the plane the ball must arrive at.
          The rocker constraint (CELLS 18.8″ apart, pivot at 43.95″, cage 12″ deep) puts it
          between 41″ and 59″; it defaults to the middle. Read Fig 9-10/9-11 or the field
          CAD before trusting an RPM off this page. Controls marked ▲ are estimates too.

        
Why arc beats power

        
    
          
  • Near-far is tight, left-right is forgiving. The CELL is 12″ deep and 20″
            wide. A ball falling at angle θ sees the depth foreshortened to
            12·sinθ but the width not at all. At 45° entry that is a
            5.7″ range window against a 17.2″ lateral window — three times more room
            sideways. Spend your effort on distance, not heading.
    • 
          
  • Too flat cannot score at all. At 15° entry the foreshortened depth is 3.1″
            and POLLEN is 2.8″ — 0.3″ of gap. No RPM precision rescues that. This is the
            same reason a coach shouts "arc".
    • 
          
  • The hood is the fingertips. A hook hood wraps the ball further round the wheel so it
            leaves rolling, near v/r of backspin. A straight ramp lets go early at about half that.
            Backspin makes Magnus lift, which flattens the rise and steepens the fall.
    • 
          
  • The tip steers, it does not lift. Yaw moves the landing point sideways along the
            20″ axis. Use it to correct heading, never to correct range.
    • 
        

        
Three ways to guess the third value

        
    
          
  • Linear regression fits one line through every point by minimising squared residuals.
            It uses all your data, so one bad shot cannot drag it far, and R² says how much scatter
            the line explains. Right when points are noisy and the truth is nearly straight.
    • 
          
  • Interpolation joins neighbours with segments. It passes exactly through every point,
            including your mistakes, and says nothing outside the measured span.
    • 
          
  • The physics model owes nothing to your data. When it disagrees with your fit, one of
            the two is wrong — a coefficient or a measurement. The panel reports that disagreement
            in inches of range.
    • 
          
  • Watch the extrapolation. Push the prediction past your furthest point and the line
            keeps going straight while the curve bends away.
    • 
        

        
The ball has holes

        
    
          
  • Perforations raise Cd — air is dragged through and behind the ball instead
            of sliding round it — so a holed ball falls shorter at the same speed. Toggle
            Smooth / Perforated and watch the required RPM move.
    • 
          
  • The holes are not symmetric, and their orientation at release changes shot to shot. So
            Cd is not one number but a spread, and that spread is a floor on accuracy that no
            amount of RPM control beats. It is the honest reason to shoot from closer.
    • 
        

        
Under the HIVE

        
    
          
  • You can drive under it. The HIVE is a sawhorse on four legs with its pivot
            43.95″ up, so an 18″ robot passes between the legs. Only the legs are solid here.
    • 
          
  • But you may not catch. G409: a ROBOT may not catch or deflect a SCORING ELEMENT
            released by a TIPPED HIVE “unless and until that SCORING ELEMENT contacts
            anything else besides that ROBOT”. Balls still in the air are not collectable
            here — the telemetry says G409 – let it land while any are falling
            near you. Once they touch the tiles they are fair game.
    • 
          
  • The manual also names repeatedly parking under the HIVE waiting for a TIP as
            likely to be judged STRATEGIC, and therefore a violation. Sweep up after the spill;
            do not camp for it.
    • 
        

        
Model limits

        
    
          
  • Fitted, not measured: transfer efficiency and Cd absorb everything the
            model ignores. Durometer preselects efficiency — 15A grips harder than 30A — but
            those are starting points. Shoot at a known distance, then tune until the page agrees.
    • 
          
  • Response uses J = 0.6·m·r² for a rim-loaded wheel and a linear
            torque curve τ = τstall(1 − ω/ωfree).
            Flywheel mass is an estimate you can override; it changes response, never trajectory.
    • 
          
  • Not modelled: the HIVE frame as an obstruction, belt losses, ball wear, or the
            manual's warning that POLLEN and NECTAR "are not perfectly spherical and may vary in size".
    • 
          
  • Prices and SKUs come from the goBILDA catalogue and your own listing screenshots.
            Every part links to its goBILDA page. 14mm-bore wheels need a hub adapter that is not in the
            BOM total.
